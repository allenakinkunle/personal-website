There is usually a notebook open beside the keyboard. It catches the things that are too early for a document: a phrase, a diagram, a question that has not yet earned an answer. The mess is useful. It gives uncertain ideas somewhere to wait.

Music does something similar. A record creates a boundary around a stretch of time. There is no endless queue and no invisible recommendation deciding what follows. You choose a side, lower the needle, and live with the sequence. That small constraint can be surprisingly generous.

Books accumulate for less orderly reasons. Some are there because I am reading them. Others are reminders of a subject I want to return to. A few remain because of one paragraph. Together they form a kind of external memory, imperfectly indexed but full of useful collisions.

The desk is not a productivity system. It is simply the landscape around the work. Over time, the objects on it reveal what keeps asking for attention.
