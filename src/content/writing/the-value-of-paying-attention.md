Attention is one of the few things we spend every day without ever seeing the balance. A notification takes some. A difficult problem takes more. By evening, we can feel the account running low even if we cannot explain where it went.

Photography has made this easier for me to notice. Walking with a camera changes the shape of an ordinary journey. Light on a wall becomes an event. A person waiting at a crossing becomes a study in posture and patience. Nothing has changed, except the quality of looking.

I think the same is true of technical work. The useful insight is often sitting in a detail that everyone has learned to step around: an awkward handoff, an unexplained spreadsheet, a decision that lives entirely in one person's memory. Paying attention is how those details become visible enough to improve.

The practice is simple, though not easy: leave a little silence before reaching for the next thing. Look twice. Make a note. The world rarely asks for more volume. It often rewards a better kind of notice.
